シェアハウス探しで「山が近い」を条件に挙げる人は、そう多くないと思います。でもThe Headquarters Kobeに住んでみると、これが思っていた以上に効いてきます。摩耶山の登山口まで、電車もバスも使わずに歩いて行けるからです。

夏、ハウスのメンバーで摩耶山に登ってきました。初めて登る人もいて、ゴールは標高690mの掬星台展望台。その日のことを書いておきます。

## 玄関を出たら、そこから登山が始まる

摩耶山は、シェアハウスから歩いて行ける距離にあります。これがどれだけ気楽なことか、住んでみるとわかります。

登山というと、朝早くに起きて、駅まで行って、電車を乗り継いで、ようやく登山口——という段取りを想像しますよね。でもここでは、その前半が丸ごと省略されます。思い立った日に靴を履いて玄関を出れば、それがもう登山の第一歩です。

だから、登山が好きな住人は毎週のように登っています。「今週も行ってくるわ」くらいのテンションで出ていく人がいるハウスです。

## 登り道は、街のすぐ裏にある森

登り始めると、すぐに景色が変わります。左手はコンクリートの擁壁、右手は落ち葉と木の根がむき出しになった斜面。足元は土と石を組んだ段になっていて、木漏れ日が落ちています。

*   ![摩耶山の登山道で、木々に囲まれた石段を登っていくシェアハウスの住人](https://theheadquarters.jp/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/fb/fb-20210816-1-1400x1400.jpg)
    

写真の通り、けっこう本格的な山道です。ついさっきまで神戸の街にいたのに、数十分歩いただけでこの緑の中にいる。この切り替わりの速さが、摩耶山の面白いところだと思います。

夏場は当然それなりに汗をかきます。それでも木陰の道なので、街を歩くよりはよほど涼しく感じます。

## 標高690m、掬星台に到着

登りきった先が、掬星台展望台です。「Mt. Maya 掬星台 Kikuseidai 690m」と書かれた木の看板があって、ここが記念撮影のポイント。初めて登ったメンバーも、ここで無事にピースサインです。

*   ![摩耶山掬星台の標高690mと書かれた木の看板の前でピースサインをする住人](https://theheadquarters.jp/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/fb/fb-20210816-2-1400x1400.jpg)
    

掬星台という名前は、それだけで景色の良さが伝わってくる響きです。ここまで自分の足で上がってきた、という達成感も込みで、休憩の時間が気持ちいい場所でした。

## 次は、帰りに灘温泉

この日、下山しながら出た話がひとつあります。「次は、帰りに灘温泉に行きたいな」。

登山のあとに温泉、という発想が自然に出てくるのは、どちらも生活圏の中にあるからです。山も銭湯も、わざわざ遠出して行くものではなく、暮らしのついでに寄れるところにある。神戸という街のサイズ感が、こういうところに出ている気がします。

この「次はこうしよう」が積み重なって、また誰かが登りに行く。ハウスの遊びはだいたいこの流れで増えていきます。

Not many people put "close to the mountains" on their list of requirements when looking for a share house. But once you live at The Headquarters Kobe, it turns out to matter more than you'd expect — because you can walk to the trailhead of Mt. Maya without taking a train or a bus.

One summer, a group of us from the house hiked up Mt. Maya. Some were doing it for the first time, and our goal was Kikuseidai, the observation deck at 690 meters. Here's a record of that day.

## The hike starts the moment you step out the door

Mt. Maya is within walking distance of the share house. You only really appreciate what an easy thing that is once you're living here.

When you picture a day of hiking, you probably imagine getting up early, heading to the station, changing trains, and finally arriving at the trailhead. Here, that entire first half gets skipped. Put your shoes on and walk out the door on a whim, and you've already taken the first step of the hike.

That's why residents who like hiking go up almost every week. This is a house where someone heads out saying, more or less, "Going up again this week."

## The trail: a forest right behind the city

The scenery changes almost as soon as you start climbing. A concrete retaining wall on the left, a slope of fallen leaves and exposed tree roots on the right. Underfoot are steps built from earth and stone, with sunlight filtering down through the trees.

*   ![A share house resident climbing stone steps surrounded by trees on the Mt. Maya trail](https://theheadquarters.jp/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/fb/fb-20210816-1-1400x1400.jpg)
    

As you can see in the photo, it's a proper mountain trail. You were in the middle of Kobe just moments ago, and a few dozen minutes of walking later you're surrounded by all this green. That speed of transition is what makes Mt. Maya fun.

In summer you'll naturally work up a sweat. Even so, the trail stays in the shade of the trees, so it feels considerably cooler than walking through the city.

## Reaching Kikuseidai at 690 meters

At the top of the climb is the Kikuseidai observation deck. There's a wooden sign reading "Mt. Maya 掬星台 Kikuseidai 690m" — the spot for the commemorative photo. Our first-time hikers made it up safely and threw a peace sign here too.

*   ![A resident making a peace sign in front of the wooden Mt. Maya Kikuseidai 690m sign](https://theheadquarters.jp/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/fb/fb-20210816-2-1400x1400.jpg)
    

The name Kikuseidai — "the place where you scoop up the stars" — tells you something about the view all on its own. With the satisfaction of having climbed up here on your own two legs, it's a lovely place to take a break.

## Next time: Nada Onsen on the way home

One idea came up on the way back down that day: "Next time I want to stop at Nada Onsen on the way home."

The reason that pairing comes up so naturally is that both the mountain and the bathhouse are part of everyday life around here. Neither is a special trip you plan around; they're both places you can drop by as part of an ordinary day. The scale of a city like Kobe shows itself in details like this.

Those "let's do it this way next time" ideas pile up, and someone heads back up the mountain again. That's more or less how the things we do around the house multiply.

The Headquarters Kobe sits right by the Suidosuji shopping street. You're never stuck for shopping, and yet you can walk straight into the mountains. That combination is, to us, what makes living in Kobe interesting.

For anyone whose hobby is hiking, the location of the house is practically part of the gear.
